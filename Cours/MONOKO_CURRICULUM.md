# MONOKO — Universal Language Curriculum

## Overview

This curriculum is the standard learning path for ALL languages on the Monoko platform.
Every language (Lingala, Yoruba, Bamileke, etc.) follows the same structure.
Content is language-specific, but the modules, progression, and exam format are universal.

The curriculum follows CEFR-inspired progression (A1 → B2+) adapted for oral-first African languages.
Each level contains themed modules. Each module contains lessons. Each lesson contains items (vocabulary, phrases, grammar rules, exercises).

inseparable from everyday speech in the target communities, and no module covered
phones, messaging or mobile money at all. They are part of the universal
curriculum, so a new language should collect them too.

## LEVEL 1 — FONDATIONS (A1 Beginner)
**Goal:** Survive basic social interactions. Understand the sound system.

### Module 1.1 — Sons et alphabet
The target language's sound system.
- Vowel inventory (open vs closed vowels, nasal vowels if applicable)
- Consonant inventory (prenasalized consonants, labio-velars if applicable)
- Tonal system (high, low, rising, falling — how tone changes meaning)
- Syllable structure (CV, CVC patterns)
- Minimal pairs demonstrating tone/sound contrasts
- Pronunciation drills: listen and repeat for every sound
- **This module is primarily audio-based. Minimal text.**

### Module 1.2 — Salutations et politesse
Greetings and basic social formulas.
- Saying hello (formal, informal, by time of day)
- Saying goodbye
- Saying please, thank you, excuse me, sorry
- Asking "how are you?" and responding
- Greeting elders with respect
- Welcoming someone
- Wishing someone well (good day, good night, good trip, happy birthday)
- **Target: 20-30 items with audio**

### Module 1.3 — Présentation personnelle
Introducing yourself and asking about others.
- Saying your name / asking someone's name
- Saying where you're from / asking where someone is from
- Saying your age
- Saying what language(s) you speak
- Saying you're learning the target language
- Saying you don't understand / understand a little
- Basic personal pronouns (I, you, he/she, we, you plural, they)
- Possessive forms (my, your, his/her)
- **Target: 25-35 items**

### Module 1.4 — Chiffres, jours et temps
Numbers, time, and calendar.
- Numbers 1-20 (individual items with audio)
- Numbers by tens: 30, 40, 50... 100
- Days of the week
- Months of the year
- Today, yesterday, tomorrow, this week, next week
- Telling time (what time is it? it's 3 o'clock, morning, afternoon, evening)
- Seasons (if applicable in the cultural context)
- **Target: 50-60 items**

### 📝 Exam 1
- **Written (40%):** Match 10 greetings to their meaning. Translate 15 basic phrases (both directions). Fill in missing numbers in a sequence.
- **Listening (30%):** Listen to 10 audio clips and select the correct meaning. Listen to someone introduce themselves and answer 5 questions.
- **Speaking (30%):** Introduce yourself in 5+ sentences (pronunciation scored). Respond to 5 audio prompts (greetings, questions about yourself).
- **Pass threshold: 70%**

## LEVEL 2 — VIE QUOTIDIENNE (A2 Elementary)
**Goal:** Handle everyday situations. Basic sentence construction.

### Module 2.1 — La famille et les relations
Family vocabulary and talking about relationships.
- Immediate family (father, mother, brother, sister, child, son, daughter)
- Extended family (uncle, aunt, grandfather, grandmother, cousin, nephew/niece)
- Spouse (husband, wife)
- Describing your family ("I have 3 brothers", "My mother lives in...")
- Asking about someone's family
- Possessive constructions with family ("my father's house", "her children")
- **Target: 30-40 items**

### Module 2.2 — La maison et les objets
Home, rooms, and household items.
- Rooms (bedroom, kitchen, bathroom, living room)
- Furniture (bed, table, chair, cupboard)
- Household objects (plate, pot, broom, bucket, key, door)
- Describing where things are (prepositions: on, under, inside, next to, in front of, behind)
- Actions at home (cook, clean, sleep, wash, open, close)
- "Where is the...?" / "It's in the..."
- **Target: 40-50 items**

### Module 2.3 — Manger et boire
Food, drinks, and meals.
- Common foods (rice, fish, chicken, cassava, plantain, beans, bread, eggs, vegetables, fruit)
- Drinks (water, juice, tea, coffee, beer, palm wine)
- Meals (breakfast, lunch, dinner)
- Cooking verbs (cook, fry, boil, grill, mix, cut, peel)
- At the market/restaurant ("I want...", "Give me...", "How much?", "It's too expensive")
- Expressing hunger, thirst, taste (hot, sweet, salty, bitter, spicy)
- **Target: 45-55 items**

### Module 2.4 — Le corps et la santé
Body parts, health, and illness.
- Body parts head to toe (head, hair, eyes, ears, nose, mouth, teeth, neck, shoulder, arm, hand, fingers, chest, stomach, back, leg, knee, foot)
- Common symptoms ("I'm sick", "I have a headache", "my stomach hurts", "I have a fever", "I'm coughing")
- Going to the doctor/hospital
- Basic medicine vocabulary
- Describing physical states (tired, strong, weak, hot, cold)
- **Target: 40-50 items**

### Module 2.5 — Construction de phrases 1
Basic sentence structure.
- Affirmative sentences (Subject + tense marker + verb + complement)
- Negative sentences (negation markers and placement)
- Yes/no questions
- Question words (who? what? where? when? why? how? how much/many?)
- Answering questions
- Word order rules
- **Target: 30-40 items with example sentences for each pattern**

### 📝 Exam 2
- **Written (40%):** Describe your family in 8+ sentences. Translate 15 sentences about daily life. Label body parts on a diagram. Fill in blanks with correct prepositions.
- **Listening (30%):** Listen to someone describe their house and draw/identify the layout. Listen to a market dialogue and answer questions about what was bought and the prices.
- **Speaking (30%):** Role-play: buy 3 items at a market (spoken dialogue scored). Describe what you ate today. Describe a family member.
- **Pass threshold: 70%**

## LEVEL 3 — COMMUNICATION (B1 Intermediate)
**Goal:** Hold real conversations. Master the verb system.

### Module 3.1 — Déplacements et directions
Travel, transportation, and navigation.
- Modes of transport (car, bus, taxi, motorcycle, bicycle, boat, plane, on foot)
- Giving directions (turn right/left, go straight, stop, it's next to..., it's far/near)
- Asking for directions ("Where is...?", "How do I get to...?")
- Travel vocabulary (road, bridge, intersection, traffic, station, airport)
- Distance and duration ("How far?", "How long does it take?")
- Common travel phrases ("I'm going to...", "I'm coming from...", "Wait for me")
- **Target: 35-45 items**

### Module 3.2 — Le travail et les métiers
Professions and workplace.
- Common professions (teacher, doctor, farmer, merchant, driver, police, cook, tailor, carpenter, mechanic, student, musician)
- Asking about someone's job ("What do you do?", "Where do you work?")
- Describing your work
- Workplace vocabulary (office, school, hospital, market, field)
- Work-related verbs (work, earn, sell, buy, build, repair, teach, learn)
- **Target: 30-40 items**

### Module 3.3 — Conjugaison — présent et passé
Verb system deep dive (part 1).
- Present tense formation and markers
- Present continuous (action happening now)
- Habitual present (things you do regularly)
- Recent past (just happened)
- Distant/completed past
- Common irregular verbs in present and past
- Practice: 15-20 high-frequency verbs conjugated across these tenses with example sentences
- **Target: 50-60 items (verb tables + example sentences)**

### Module 3.4 — Conjugaison — futur et impératif
Verb system deep dive (part 2).
- Near future (about to happen)
- Distant future (will happen)
- Imperative/commands (positive: "do this!", negative: "don't do that!")
- Requests and suggestions ("Can you...?", "Let's...", "You should...")
- Conditional ("If... then...")
- Practice: same 15-20 verbs conjugated in future and imperative
- **Target: 40-50 items**

### Module 3.5 — Sentiments et émotions
Expressing feelings and opinions.
- Emotions (happy, sad, angry, afraid, surprised, worried, ashamed, proud, jealous, tired, bored)
- Physical states (hungry, thirsty, hot, cold, sick, in pain)
- Expressing likes and dislikes ("I like...", "I don't like...", "I love...", "I hate...")
- Expressing agreement and disagreement
- Expressing certainty and doubt ("Maybe", "I think that...", "I'm sure that...")
- Compliments and encouragement
- **Target: 35-45 items**

### Module 3.6 — Construction de phrases 2
Advanced sentence patterns.
- Conjunctions (and, but, because, therefore, if, when, while, although, so that)
- Relative clauses ("the person who...", "the thing that...")
- Comparatives and superlatives (more than, less than, the most, the same as)
- Frequency adverbs (always, sometimes, never, often, rarely)
- Probability and possibility expressions
- Linking sentences into paragraphs
- **Target: 35-45 items**

### 📝 Exam 3
- **Written (40%):** Describe your typical workday using correct tenses. Conjugate 10 verbs across 3 tenses. Translate 10 complex sentences with conjunctions. Give directions from point A to point B.
- **Listening (30%):** Listen to a 2-minute dialogue and summarize it. Listen to someone describe their job and answer questions. Identify the tense used in 10 spoken sentences.
- **Speaking (30%):** Describe how you're feeling and why (1 minute). Give directions to a location. Tell what you did yesterday and what you'll do tomorrow.
- **Pass threshold: 70%**

## LEVEL 4 — APPROFONDISSEMENT (B1+)
**Goal:** Build fluency and cultural understanding.

### Module 4.1 — Le marché et l'argent
Advanced commercial interactions.
- Extended market dialogues (negotiating price, quantities, quality)
- Money vocabulary (price, change, expensive, cheap, discount, debt, savings)
- Banking basics (if culturally relevant)
- Buying and selling scenarios
- Expressing quantities precisely
- Idiomatic commercial expressions
- **Target: 30-40 items**

### Module 4.2 — La nature et les animaux
Natural world vocabulary.
- Domestic animals (dog, cat, chicken, goat, cow, pig)
- Wild animals (lion, elephant, monkey, snake, crocodile, bird, fish, insect)
- Nature vocabulary (tree, flower, river, mountain, forest, rain, sun, moon, stars, earth, sky)
- Weather expressions ("It's raining", "It's hot", "The sun is shining")
- Seasons and agricultural cycle (if applicable)
- Environmental descriptions
- **Target: 45-55 items**

### Module 4.3 — Proverbes et expressions idiomatiques
Cultural wisdom and figurative language.
- 15-20 common proverbs with literal translation and meaning
- Idiomatic expressions used in daily speech
- Figurative language (metaphors common in the culture)
- When and how to use proverbs in conversation
- Cultural context behind key expressions
- **This module requires native speaker / professor input**
- **Target: 25-35 items**

### Module 4.4 — Raconter une histoire
Narrative skills.
- Sequencing words (first, then, after that, finally, suddenly, meanwhile)
- Past tenses in narrative context (switching between background and foreground)
- Describing people and places in detail
- Dialogue within stories (reported speech)
- Traditional story openings and closings
- Practice: retell a folk tale or personal anecdote
- **Target: 25-35 items + 2-3 sample stories**

### Module 4.5 — La ville et les lieux
Urban and geographic vocabulary.
- City landmarks (church, mosque, school, hospital, market, stadium, government building)
- Neighborhood vocabulary (street, intersection, block, neighborhood)
- Describing where you live
- Urban vs rural life comparisons
- Geographic terms (country, city, village, capital, border)
- Clothing and appearance vocabulary (shirt, pants, shoes, dress, hat, bag — for describing people)
- **Target: 40-50 items**

### 📝 Exam 4
- **Written (40%):** Write a dialogue of 15+ exchanges at a market with negotiation. Explain the meaning of 5 proverbs. Describe an animal without naming it (partner guesses).
- **Listening (30%):** Listen to a folk tale (3 minutes) and answer comprehension questions. Listen to a weather report and summarize it.
- **Speaking (30%):** Tell a 2-minute story about a memory. Use 3 proverbs correctly in context. Describe your neighborhood.
- **Pass threshold: 70%**

## LEVEL 5 — MAÎTRISE (B2)
**Goal:** Handle nuance, register, and extended discourse.

### Module 5.1 — Registres: formel vs informel
Language registers and social appropriateness.
- Formal vs informal greetings and address
- Respectful language for elders and authority figures
- Professional/workplace register
- Youth slang and casual speech
- Regional variations (e.g., Kinshasa vs Brazzaville Lingala, or Lagos vs Ibadan Yoruba)
- Code-switching with French/English (when and how)
- **Target: 25-35 items with paired formal/informal examples**

### Module 5.2 — Débats et opinions
Argumentation and discussion.
- Expressing strong opinions ("I believe that...", "In my view...")
- Agreeing partially ("You're right, but...")
- Disagreeing politely
- Presenting arguments (firstly, secondly, on the other hand)
- Expressing cause and effect
- Persuading someone
- **Target: 30-40 items**

### Module 5.3 — Médias et actualités
Understanding media and current affairs.
- News vocabulary (event, war, peace, election, government, law)
- Understanding radio broadcasts (adapted audio clips)
- Music lyric analysis (understanding Lingala rumba, Yoruba jùjú, etc.)
- Social media language and abbreviations
- Reading comprehension of adapted texts
- **Target: 25-35 items + 3-5 adapted texts/audio**

### Module 5.4 — Écriture et composition
Written expression.
- Writing a personal letter / message
- Writing a formal letter
- Common written abbreviations and conventions
- Orthographic rules and spelling patterns
- Paragraph structure and coherence
- Short essay writing
- **Target: 20-30 items + writing templates**

### 📝 Exam 5
- **Written (40%):** Write a formal letter requesting something. Read an adapted news article and summarize in target language. Rewrite 5 informal sentences in formal register.
- **Listening (30%):** Listen to a 3-minute radio clip and answer comprehension questions. Listen to a debate between two people and identify each person's position.
- **Speaking (30%):** Debate a simple topic for 3 minutes (scored on fluency, grammar, pronunciation). Explain a current event.
- **Pass threshold: 70%**

## LEVEL 6 — CULTURE VIVANTE (B2+)
**Goal:** Deep cultural connection. Near-native comprehension.

### Module 6.1 — Musique et arts
Music, dance, and artistic expression.
- Major musical genres and their cultural significance
- Understanding and discussing song lyrics (themes, metaphors)
- Dance vocabulary and cultural context
- Artistic traditions (sculpture, painting, weaving — if applicable)
- Discussing what you like about a song or performance
- **Target: 20-30 items + 3-5 song excerpts with analysis**

### Module 6.2 — Cuisine et gastronomie
Advanced food culture.
- Traditional recipes with step-by-step instructions
- Regional specialty dishes and their cultural significance
- Cooking techniques and kitchen vocabulary
- Food-related proverbs and expressions
- Describing tastes and textures in detail
- Hosting guests and food etiquette
- **Target: 25-35 items + 3-5 complete recipes**

### Module 6.3 — Traditions et cérémonies
Cultural practices and celebrations.
- Wedding customs and vocabulary
- Naming ceremonies
- Funeral and mourning customs
- Religious and spiritual expressions
- Traditional celebrations and festivals
- Rites of passage
- **Target: 25-35 items**

### Module 6.4 — La langue dans le monde
The language in its modern, global context.
- Diaspora communities and how the language evolves abroad
- Modern slang and neologisms
- The language in digital spaces (texting, social media)
- Borrowings from other languages and vice versa
- Language preservation and why it matters
- Resources for continued learning beyond Monoko
- **Target: 20-30 items**

### Module 6.5 — Religion et spiritualité
Faith, ritual, and spiritual vocabulary — central to daily life across the target
communities and heavily represented in music, greetings, and set expressions.
- Places of worship and their role in community life
- Clergy, congregation, and the vocabulary of a service
- Prayer, blessing, and common devotional formulas
- Traditional beliefs alongside imported religions, and how they coexist
- Religious festivals and the language used around them
- **Target: 20-30 items**

### Module 6.6 — Technologie et communication
Modern digital life, which older curricula omit entirely and learners need daily.
- Phone, internet, and messaging vocabulary
- Social media and how the language behaves online
- Sending, receiving, sharing — the verbs of digital exchange
- Money transfer and mobile payment (dominant in the target markets)
- Neologisms and borrowings for technology that has no traditional term
- **Target: 20-30 items**

### 📝 Exam final
- **Written (30%):** Translate a 150-word passage from French to target language. Write a cultural essay (200+ words) on a tradition. Explain 5 proverbs/idioms.
- **Listening (30%):** Listen to a 5-minute natural conversation and answer detailed questions. Listen to a song and explain its themes.
- **Speaking (40%):** 10-minute free conversation with Monoko AI on a randomly assigned topic (holistic scoring: fluency, grammar, pronunciation, cultural appropriateness, vocabulary range).
- **Pass threshold: 70%**

## EXAM SYSTEM SPECIFICATIONS — SUPERSEDED 2026-08-07

> **This whole section is historical.** Exams were dropped in favour of
> continuous points on every exercise; all levels are open and the paywall is the
> only gate. Of what follows, only the pronunciation feature survives — as a
> normal exercise type, built last, gated on measuring real Lingala STT accuracy
> (`api/elevenlabs-stt.js` documents 20–50% WER). See `EXERCISE_ENGINE_PLAN.md`.

### Three components per exam:
1. **Written** — Translation, fill-in-the-blank, sentence construction, short writing
2. **Listening** — Audio comprehension (from recorded native speaker content or TTS)
3. **Speaking** — User records responses; scored by AI via speech-to-text comparison + LLM evaluation

### Scoring:
- Each component weighted as specified per exam
- Pass threshold: 70% overall
- User must pass to unlock next level
- Failed components can be retried individually
- Items answered incorrectly are added to a spaced repetition review queue

### Pronunciation feature (per lesson):
- Every lesson item has a "Prononce" button
- User records themselves via microphone
- Speech-to-text (ElevenLabs Scribe) transcribes what they said
- App compares transcription to expected text
- Score displayed as match percentage
- Future enhancement: phoneme-level and tone-level feedback

## CONTENT MAPPING NOTES

When restructuring existing content into this curriculum:
- The same item can appear in multiple modules if contextually relevant
- Dictionary entries (words table) can be promoted to lesson_items when they fit a module theme
- Example sentences from the dictionary are especially valuable as lesson content
- Modules marked "requires professor input" should be created as empty shells with placeholder items
- Each item should have: french text, target language text, example sentence in target language, example sentence translation
- Audio fields should be left empty for now — they'll be populated in a later phase

## DATABASE MAPPING

This curriculum maps to the existing Supabase schema:
- Each LEVEL = a `course` row (course_order = level number)
- Each MODULE = a `lesson` row (parent_theme = level title, lesson_order = module number)
- Each ITEM = a `lesson_item` row (item_order preserves sequence within module)
- The `language_id` foreign key makes this structure reusable across all languages
